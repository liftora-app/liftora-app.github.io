How Vectra handles your data, and how to reach us

Vectra is a small workout-tracking app. This page explains who runs it, what it stores and why, who can see it, how long it is kept, how to delete it, and your rights.

Support

Vectra is run by Lars Sarmiento, Oslo, Norway.

Email: larssarmiento@gmail.com

Write to that address for anything: a bug, a question about your data, a request to delete something, or to report a person, a post or a crew. The operator aims to read and act on reports of abuse within 24 hours, reports about someone's safety first; a report that needs more looking into can take longer. Everything else gets an answer within a few days.

How to delete your account

In the app: Profile → Settings → Delete account, then enter your password to confirm. This removes everything about you from Vectra in one step (see Deleting below). You do not need to email us, but you can if the app is not working for you, and we will delete the account by hand.

How to report someone or something

On a post or a profile, tap … and choose Report post or Report account; on a comment, press and hold it and choose Report comment. On a crew's page, tap … and choose Report this crew (as a member: … then Report this crew in the crew's settings); this is also how you report a crew's cover photo or icon. Then pick a reason. You can also block an account from its profile, from the … menu on one of its posts, or by pressing and holding one of its comments; blocked accounts cannot follow you or see your workouts; we do not notify them, but they may notice what they can no longer see or do. If the app's own buttons are not enough, email us with a description of what you saw and, if you have it, the person's friend code or display name.

TestFlight and the App Store

If you install Vectra through Apple's TestFlight, Apple collects crash logs and usage information from TestFlight testers and shares them with us; if you were invited by email, that includes your name and email address. We use this only to fix the app. Apple's own description of what TestFlight shares is at apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight. To see or delete the TestFlight data we hold about you, email us.

If you install Vectra from the App Store, crash reports are handled by Apple, not by Vectra. Apple may share crash data and statistics about how you use the app with us. Where they hold anything about you, we use it only to fix the app. To stop that sharing, go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements on your iPhone and turn off Share With App Developers.

Why Vectra is allowed to use your data

European data-protection law lets Vectra use your data only on one of the grounds it lists. Vectra uses four, each for the uses it names:

  • To run the app you signed up for (performance of a contract: the terms of use below). This covers what you use the app for: your account and signing you in; keeping your workout history and what goes with it (templates, exercise notes, favourites, progress resets, badges and the gyms you add); showing your profile, posts, photos, videos, badges, fist bumps and comments to the people "Who can see what" names, your follows and requests, your notifications, and keeping you apart from the people you block; the crews you create, join or ask to join, and the posts you share to them; letting the people who can see your posts copy a workout from them with Copy split (terms item 3); and what the app keeps on your phone (see "On your phone"). It also covers answering what you write to us about your account. To have an account you must give an email address, a password, and a first and last name; everything else you give Vectra is up to you.
  • To keep Vectra working and secure, and stop abuse (legitimate interests: ours and our members'). This covers using what Apple shares with us through TestFlight and the App Store to fix the app, the record of each sign-in, Supabase's service logs, reports and the copy each one keeps, the attempt log that limits how many follow requests, comments, reports and requests to join a crew you can send, an owner's record of a lift they left off the board, until its week is over, and checking names, short text, comments, a crew's name and description, and posts against the word list (see "Filtered content"). It also covers answering what you write to us that is not about your account. Legitimate interests are also why a Copy split copy of your workout keeps your name as it was then, for the member who made it, even after you delete your account. You can object to these uses (see "Your rights").
  • Your permission (consent), where iOS asks for it: your location, for the gym search; the camera, for a profile picture, a workout photo or a friend's code; adding pictures to your photo library with Save Image; and notifications, for an alert when a rest is over. You can turn them off at any time in the iPhone's Settings → Notifications → Vectra, or turn off Rest is over in Vectra's Settings. You can withdraw it at any time in the iPhone's Settings → Privacy & Security, under Location Services, Camera or Photos. From then on Vectra cannot use it. What you already added with it, such as a gym or a photo on a post, is the app's own data under the first item and stays until it is deleted: a photo when you remove it or delete its post, a gym with your account, or sooner if you email us (see How long data is kept, and how to delete it). The photo picker needs no permission: iOS shows it, and gives Vectra only what you pick. Your permission is also the ground for a crew's board (see "A crew's board"): you give it by turning "Show me on the board" on in that crew, and it is withdrawn when you turn it off, leave the crew or are removed from it.
  • To do what the law requires (legal obligation): answering a request to use your rights below, and giving data to an authority that has the legal power to demand it.

Location, camera and photos

Vectra reads your location only when you tap the gym field after a workout, to suggest gyms near you. Your position is sent to Supabase at full precision for that search and is not stored, unless you add a new gym, in which case it is stored rounded to three decimal places as the gym's position. The camera opens when you set a profile picture, add a photo to a workout or scan a friend's code; the photo picker opens only when you set a profile picture, add photos or a video to a workout, or set the cover photo or icon of a crew you own. To add a video you choose one from your photo library; Vectra does not record video. Vectra receives only the pictures you take or choose and the video you choose, never the rest of your photo library. Share as overlay, after a workout or in your own post's … menu, offers six ready-made overlays to pick from, in white or black. Each is a see-through picture of a title and the volume, time and number of personal records, made on your phone at full story size (1080 by 1920 pixels); one of them, V-taper, also draws a small bar loaded to about your top weight. From your post, the title is the post's title. After a workout, it is the workout's name as you started it (for a template of your own, the name you gave it), not a title you typed for the post, and it is not checked against the word list. The picture is never sent to Vectra's servers, and it leaves the app only if you share it. Share opens the iPhone's share sheet, where Save Image adds it to your photo library, which iOS asks your permission for, and where else you send it is your choice. A scanned code is sent to Supabase only to find that person, exactly as a typed code is. It is not saved in Vectra's database; like every request, it can appear for a short time in Supabase's service logs (see How long data is kept). No background location, no motion tracking, no contacts, no microphone. A video you add keeps the sound it was recorded with: the sound is sent with the video and kept with it.

On your phone

Vectra keeps these on your phone: your sign-in session (a token, with your email address and the first and last name you typed at sign-up); your theme, weight unit and favourites order, the Workouts tab you last opened, the Sound and Vibration choices and the machine you last used for cardio intervals, which loading animation shows next, and whether you dismissed the reps-in-reserve tip; a workout or a challenge level in progress, and challenge workouts not yet sent, for example because the phone was offline; a note when the app saves or discards a workout you left open, with your account's id; a cached exercise list, and cached copies of the pictures, photos and videos shown to you; an overlay picture made by Share, until you share again or close the Share as overlay sheet (if the app is quit first, until iOS clears the app's cache); and whether rest alerts are on for this phone and whether you tapped Not now on the card that offers them (both shared by every account on the phone), and, while you rest, one alert scheduled on the phone for the moment the rest ends ("Rest over", "Time for your next set."), cancelled when you skip or shorten the rest, start a timed set, post, save or discard the workout, or log out. The rest alert is made and shown by your phone: nothing about it is sent to Vectra's servers or to anyone else. A challenge level in progress, challenge workouts not yet sent and the Workouts tab you last opened are kept under your account's id and stay after you log out, for when that account logs in on that phone again; another account on the same phone never sees or sends them. The Sound and Vibration choices and the last machine belong to the phone, shared by every account on it. A video you have watched stays on your phone until you sign out, delete your account from that phone, or your phone clears the app's cache; while it is there it can play without Vectra asking the server again.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Everything above is kept for as long as your account exists, except that attempt-log entries are deleted within two days; a record of a lift left off a crew's board is deleted within a day after its week ends; leaving a crew, or being removed or banned, deletes your membership and your shares there; a post you delete is gone at once, and a photo, video or picture you remove or replace, or that was on a post you delete, is deleted at once; if that fails, the app deletes it later, on a later day when you next use Vectra or, for a crew's pictures, when its owner next opens Edit crew. To delete one of your posts, tap … on it in your feed and choose Delete post: it is deleted with its photos and video and with every fist bump and comment on it, other people's too, and people who could see it stop seeing it the next time their app asks for it; a photo their phone already downloaded may stay there for a while, as with any photo shown on a phone, and a video they already watched can play again on their phone until it is cleared there (see "On your phone"). The workout stays in your history, where you can post it again; copies other members made of it with Copy split stay theirs; and a report about the post or one of its comments keeps its copy of what was reported. An owner deletes a crew in Edit crew: its cover photo and icon, member list, join requests, bans, board settings and shares go with it at once; the shared posts stay on their owners' feeds. A badge cannot be deleted on its own, not even by deleting a post of the workout that earned it; it is kept for as long as your account exists. The operator promises to delete closed reports by hand once a month, once they were filed more than 90 days earlier; until then the database keeps them. Delete account in Settings (enter your password to confirm) removes it all in one step: your workouts, your badges and their copies, posts and the photos and videos on them, templates, favourites, exercise notes, the gyms you added, your picture, and your login. It also removes your profile card, your follow requests and follows in both directions, blocks you made and blocks others made of you, the fist bumps and comments you gave and the ones others left on your posts, the reports you filed and the reports others filed about you, the crews you own, with their pictures, member lists, join requests, bans, board settings and the shares of posts to them (the posts stay on their owners' feeds); your memberships and join requests in other crews, the posts you shared there and bans other owners made of you; your attempt log, and your notification record. Your sign-in session, a workout in progress, the picture, photo and video cache, your saved unit, your favourites order, the Workouts tab you last opened, the note about a workout the app closed for you, a challenge level in progress and the challenge workouts still waiting to be sent are cleared on the phone you delete from; your theme choice, the cached exercise list, the loading-animation order, whether you dismissed the reps-in-reserve tip, the Sound and Vibration choices and last machine for cardio intervals, whether rest alerts are on, and the card's Not now are not about you and stay, and an overlay picture left in the app's cache stays until iOS clears it. You can also email us to have the account deleted.

If you delete a post or your account from an older version of the app, a video is handled by the newest version: update Vectra first. An older version leaves a deleted post's video files stored, where no other member can see them, until the newest version deletes them the next time you start it or sign in and your feed loads, once they are more than a day old, and it cannot delete your account while any of your video files is stored: it first deletes your profile picture and the photos on your posts, then says that some files could not be removed.

Six things Delete account does not reach:

  • Supabase's service logs, which record your email address, your phone's internet address, a rough location derived from that address, a description of your device, and when you signed in and used the app. They can also hold the address of each request, which can include a name or friend code you searched. On our current plan Supabase keeps its API and database logs, and the sign-in audit log, for 7 days.
  • Backups. Supabase keeps a daily backup of the database for 7 days, so data you delete stays in those backups for up to 7 days and then is gone. The backups hold the database only, not the photos, videos and pictures themselves. The operator uses a backup only to recover the service from a fault, never in order to bring back data someone deleted. If a backup ever has to be restored, data deleted after it was taken comes back with it, and the operator then deletes again everything deleted in that time, as far as the database, as the fault left it, still shows what that was. If you changed or cleared your name, your short text, or a post's title or note in that time, that text goes back to how it was when the backup was taken.
  • Your friend code is retired, not deleted: it stays in the database linked to no account, nobody can look it up, and it is never given to anyone else.
  • Copies of your workouts that other members made with Copy split: their template keeps the post's title, exercises, set counts and the name you had at that time, as text, with no link to your account.
  • What Vectra keeps on another phone where you were signed in: your saved unit, your favourites order, the Workouts tab you last opened, a challenge level in progress and any workout still waiting to be sent stay on that phone, stored under your account's id, until the app is deleted from it, and the cached pictures and photos until iOS clears them. A workout still waiting to be sent can never be saved, because the account is gone.
  • Email you sent us. What you wrote to larssarmiento@gmail.com, and our replies, stay in the operator's Gmail account until the operator deletes them. The operator deletes a conversation once the matter is closed, or at once if you ask.

Your rights

European data-protection law gives you the rights below, and using them costs nothing, unless a request is clearly unfounded or excessive: then the law lets us charge a reasonable fee or refuse it. We answer a request without undue delay and within one month of getting it. If a request is complex, or we get many at once, we may take up to two months more; if so, we tell you why within the first month. Where a right is used by email, write to larssarmiento@gmail.com, from the email address of your Vectra account if you can (for TestFlight data, the address you were invited with), so we know the request is yours. If you cannot, say so, and we will find another way to check.

  • See your data and get a copy (access). Most of it is in the app. For a copy of everything Vectra holds about you, email us.
  • Correct it (rectification). Change much of it in the app, under Edit profile and in Settings, a post's title and note with Edit title and note, and a crew you own in Edit crew. For anything you cannot correct in the app, email us.
  • Delete it (erasure). Delete a post, or a crew you own in Edit crew, in the app, or everything at once with Delete account (see How long data is kept, and how to delete it). To have anything else deleted, email us.
  • Limit how it is used (restriction). Ask us to keep some of your data but stop using it, for example while we check a correction you asked for or an objection you made. Email us.
  • Object (objection). You can object to the uses under "To keep Vectra working and secure, and stop abuse" above. Email us and say why; we then stop, unless we can show compelling reasons that outweigh your interests and rights, or need the data for a legal claim.
  • Take your data elsewhere (data portability). Email us, and we send you the data you gave Vectra in a common file format that other apps can read, or, where that can be done, straight to another service you name.
  • Withdraw your permission (withdrawing consent). Turn off Vectra's access to your location, the camera or your photo library in the iPhone's Settings, or notifications in Settings → Notifications → Vectra, or turn "Show me on the board" off in a crew's settings (the owner: in the crew's board settings), as "Why Vectra is allowed to use your data" says. Turning it off does not undo what was done before.
  • Complain. You can complain to Datatilsynet, the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (datatilsynet.no), or to the data-protection authority where you live or work, or where you think the law was broken. You can also write to us first.
